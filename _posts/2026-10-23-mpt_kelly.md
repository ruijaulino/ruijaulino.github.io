# Risk penalty meaning

In traditional mean–variance optimization, the risk-aversion parameter $\lambda$ looks like a parameter that we adjust until the portfolio seems reasonable. But through growth-optimal investing, it acquires a concrete meaning.
Consider

$$
\max_w\left(w^\top\mu-\frac{\lambda}{2}w^\top\Sigma w\right)
$$

where $\mu$ contains expected returns and $\Sigma$ is the covariance matrix. Without constraints, the solution is

$$
w_\lambda=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Sigma^{-1}\mu
$$

The allocation that maximizes expected growth rate is

$$
w_K=\Sigma^{-1}\mu
$$

Therefore,
$$
w_\lambda=\frac{1}{\lambda}w_K
$$

The risk penalty determines the fraction of Kelly exposure we take. Choosing $\lambda=2$ means half Kelly: half of each risky position in the growth-optimal portfolio. This gives $\lambda$ a natural reference interpretation. At $\lambda=1$, we take full growth-optimal exposure. At $\lambda>1$, we deliberately reduce that exposure, accepting lower expected growth in exchange for smaller fluctuations in wealth.

The coefficient multiplying variance therefore represents a concrete decision about position size:

$$
\text{Kelly fraction}=\frac{1}{\lambda}
$$

Also, it implies that $\lambda>1$ otherwise we are increasing risk for a lower growth.
